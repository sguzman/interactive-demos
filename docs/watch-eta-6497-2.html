DOCUMENTATION · PROVENANCE · M2

ETA / Unitas 6497-2

A movement-first interactive reconstruction of a large hand-wound mechanical watch. M2 focuses on bridge/plate fidelity, explicit bridge screw patterns, keyless works, bridge jewels, and more believable wheel geometry.

Accuracy contract.

The demo is not manufacturing CAD. Every important component should declare whether it is official, reference-derived, approximate, or presentational. More realism is not allowed to erase that distinction.

M2: what changed

  • Done: barrel, train, pallet and balance bridges are separate inspectable objects.
  • Done: official bridge screw counts are represented: barrel 3, train 2, pallet 2, balance 1.
  • Done: train-bridge jewels align with the third, seconds and escape-wheel pivots.
  • Done: a balance shock setting and regulator indication are visible on the balance bridge.
  • Done: dial-side winding pinion, sliding pinion, setting wheel, minute wheel, yoke and setting lever are now represented.
  • Done: crown-wheel ring and click spring are explicit parts.
  • Done: general gear teeth are tapered and the escape wheel has visibly asymmetric hooked teeth.
  • Done: wheel arbors are visible.
  • Done: camera presets jump to bridge side, dial side, escapement and winding close-ups.
  • Still approximate: exact bridge contours, jewel-seat diameters, wheel tooth counts, centre distances and keyless-part outlines.

Official parts backbone

M2 uses ETA's spare-parts list as the canonical naming spine. Important positions include main plate (1), escape wheel (12), third wheel (13), seconds wheel (14), centre wheel (15), train bridge (16), barrel (17), barrel bridge (18), crown-wheel ring (19), crown wheel (20), click spring (21), click (22), ratchet wheel (23), driver cannon pinion (24), pallet fork (25), pallet bridge (26), regulated balance (27), balance bridge (28), and hour wheel (29).

Bridge constraints

Teardown and parts documentation give a concrete visual skeleton that M2 now respects:

  • barrel bridge — 3 screws;
  • train wheel bridge — 2 screws;
  • pallet bridge — 2 screws;
  • balance bridge — 1 screw;
  • train bridge — three visible jewels for the third, seconds/fourth and escape-wheel pivots.

Constructive geometry

Authored geometry is expressed in millimetres and generated from reusable primitives. The current vocabulary includes disc, ring, roundedPlate, caseRing, polygonPlate, plateWithHoles, gear, escapeWheel, pinion, screw, jewel, shockSetting, coil, pathTube, and parameterized hands.

Bridge holes are now constructive geometry rather than merely dark circles drawn on top. Boolean CSG remains deferred until it materially helps with plate recesses, screw seats, bridge undercuts, or case geometry.

Provenance classes

official Dimension or fact directly supported by technical material.

reference-derived Reconstructed from service diagrams, teardown photographs, or multiple references.

approximate Simplified geometry preserving function and relative placement.

presentation Geometry added for readability or visual communication.

Functional assemblies

  1. Case / protection — case, crystal, exhibition back and crown guard.
  2. Display — dial and hands.
  3. Motion works — driver cannon pinion and hour wheel.
  4. Winding / keyless works — dial-side crown-position mechanism plus bridge-side crown wheel, ratchet, click and spring.
  5. Power — barrel and mainspring.
  6. Wheel train — centre, third, seconds/fourth and escape wheels with visible arbors.
  7. Escapement — escape wheel, pallet fork and pallet stones.
  8. Oscillator / regulation — balance, hairspring, balance bridge, shock setting and regulator indication.
  9. Structure — mainplate, individual bridges, jewels and screws.

Next milestones

M3 — train fidelity

Derive better wheel and pinion dimensions, centre distances and tooth-count relationships from references; make the small-seconds output more exact; and replace independent decorative train speeds with a gear-ratio graph.

M4 — winding fidelity

Make the stem position stateful, distinguish winding from hand-setting, transmit crown rotation through the keyless works and ratchet, animate the click as a one-way constraint, and store normalized mainspring energy.

M5 — escapement fidelity

Improve escape-wheel tooth geometry, locking/impulse faces, banking, roller-jewel interaction, balance staff and hairspring geometry.

M6 — system simulation

Unify barrel energy, gear ratios, escapement release, balance state and hand positions into one causal model.
