DOCUMENTATION · PROVENANCE · M3A

ETA / Unitas 6497-2

A movement-first interactive reconstruction of a large hand-wound mechanical watch. M2 established the bridge/plate and keyless-work pass; M3a begins replacing decorative train animation with reference-derived mechanical timing.

Accuracy contract.

The demo is not manufacturing CAD. Every important component should declare whether it is official, reference-derived, approximate, or presentational. More realism is not allowed to erase that distinction.

M2: structural fidelity pass

  • Done: separate barrel, train, pallet and balance bridges.
  • Done: bridge screw counts represented as barrel 3, train 2, pallet 2, balance 1.
  • Done: train-bridge jewels aligned with third, seconds and escape-wheel pivots.
  • Done: balance shock setting and regulator indication.
  • Done: dial-side winding pinion, sliding pinion, setting wheel, minute wheel, yoke and setting lever.
  • Done: crown-wheel ring, click spring, explicit wheel arbors, tapered general gear teeth and asymmetric escape-wheel teeth.

M3a: reference train kinematics

The train is no longer animated with arbitrary independent speeds. A new kinematics.js module derives wheel periods from a published 6497 training-tool ratio set and validates the ratios at startup.

Escape1 rev / 5 s
Seconds / fourth1 rev / 60 s
Third1 rev / 7.5 min
Centre1 rev / 1 h
Balance3 Hz
Beat rate6 beats / s

The reference train data reports a 15-tooth escape wheel with a 10-leaf pinion, a 120-tooth seconds/fourth wheel with an 8-leaf pinion, a 60-tooth third wheel with a 10-leaf pinion, and an 80-tooth centre wheel. The source explicitly discusses a training-tool version, so these values are treated as reference-derived, not official production tooth counts for every 6497-2 variant.

M3a also fixes a conceptual error in the early animation: the Swiss lever releases the escape wheel by half a tooth per beat, not one full tooth. At six beats per second and 15 escape teeth, that yields 30 beat steps per revolution, or five seconds per escape-wheel revolution.

Current model boundary

The timing graph is now mechanically constrained, but the visible wheel radii, centre distances and some displayed tooth counts are still M2 geometry. M3b will bring the visible train closer to the kinematic reference instead of quietly implying that the current meshes are exact.

Constructive geometry

Authored geometry is expressed in millimetres and generated from reusable primitives: disc, ring, roundedPlate, caseRing, polygonPlate, plateWithHoles, gear, escapeWheel, pinion, screw, jewel, shockSetting, coil, pathTube, and parameterized hands.

Provenance classes

official Dimension or fact directly supported by technical material.

reference-derived Reconstructed from service diagrams, teardown photographs, or multiple references.

approximate Simplified geometry preserving function and relative placement.

presentation Geometry added for readability or visual communication.

Next work

M3b — train geometry fidelity

Align visible wheel/pinion tooth counts with the reference set where defensible, add the missing escape pinion explicitly, improve wheel heights/staffs, and solve better centre distances rather than hand-placing gears.

M4 — winding fidelity

Make crown/stem position stateful, distinguish winding from hand-setting, transmit crown rotation through the keyless works and ratchet, animate the click as a one-way constraint, and store mainspring energy.

M5 — escapement fidelity

Model locking and impulse faces, banking and roller-jewel interaction so escape-wheel motion becomes causal rather than beat-indexed.

M6 — system simulation

Unify barrel energy, train ratios, escapement release, balance state and hand positions into one causal model.
