DOCUMENTATION · PROVENANCE · M5B

ETA / Unitas 6497-2

M5b makes the Swiss lever geometry itself inspectable: explicit pallet lock and impulse faces, banking limits, safety clearances, contact/drop markers, and event-by-event stepping layered on top of M5a's causal half-tooth release.

Accuracy contract.

This remains an educational reconstruction, not manufacturing CAD or a rigid-body contact simulation. M5b's lock depth, draw, drop and clearance values are deliberately labeled reconstruction targets rather than ETA production tolerances.

Earlier causal foundation

M3 established the train topology and geometry diagnostics. M4 added persistent crown winding, separate winding/setting stem states, stored mainspring reserve and an energy gate that stops an unwound movement. M5a then changed the escapement into a six-beat-per-second event sequence where the 15-tooth escape wheel releases one half-tooth per beat and released escape angle meters downstream train progress.

M5b — explicit pallet faces

Each pallet stone now carries two colored educational overlays: a blue locking face and a gold impulse face. They move with the fork and make it visually obvious that “lock” and “impulse” refer to different functional surface regions rather than one generic ruby block.

The face shapes are reference-informed presentation geometry. They are not yet measured ETA production polygons.

Banking and safety geometry

The current pallet travel is exposed as a banking arc of approximately ±7.7° about neutral. The roller table / guard roller / impulse jewel from M5a remain visible, while M5b adds a fork-slot envelope and clearance ring so the horn, dart and roller relationships can be inspected instead of merely named.

Lock depth, draw and drop

M5b promotes three escapement ideas to first-class diagnostic values:

Lock depth1.8° target
Draw12.0° target
Drop2.2° target

These values are intentionally reconstruction targets. Lock depth reduces through release; the drop diagnostic appears around unlock/relock transitions; the draw target is displayed as a persistent design parameter awaiting better measured geometry.

Contact and drop markers

The optional pallet-face / safety diagnostic overlay shows an active contact marker on the current entry or exit side and a secondary drop marker around the reconstructed transition region. The goal is to make the event state machine spatially inspectable and easy to challenge later.

Event stepping

M5a's Step one beat control remains. M5b adds Step next event. At 0× paused, the latter moves mechanical time only far enough to cross the next state boundary:

  1. lock → unlock;
  2. unlock → impulse;
  3. impulse → relock;
  4. relock → the next beat.

This makes the escapement teachable frame by frame instead of forcing the user to infer a fast mechanism from continuous animation.

What M5b still does not claim

  • measured ETA entry/exit pallet coordinates;
  • production lock depth, draw, drop or banking-pin dimensions;
  • exact roller-jewel path and fork-slot/horn clearances;
  • pallet/escape friction or lubrication behavior;
  • impulse energy transfer into balance amplitude;
  • a balance amplitude solved from spring torque and losses;
  • production force transmission through the train.

Useful inspection workflow

Wind the watch a little, switch mechanical time to 0× paused, open the Escapement camera view, enable pallet-face / safety diagnostics, then repeatedly press Step next event. The center-line guides can be enabled separately.
