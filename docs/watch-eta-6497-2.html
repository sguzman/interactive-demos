ETA / Unitas 6497-2

A movement-first interactive reconstruction of a large hand-wound mechanical watch. M3c gives the train internally constrained pitch geometry and turns the lighting into a genuinely usable camera-axis inspection system.

Accuracy contract.

The demo is not manufacturing CAD. Every important fact or geometry claim remains distinguishable as official, reference-derived, approximate, or presentational.

M2 — structural fidelity

Separate barrel, train, pallet and balance bridges; documented bridge screw counts; bridge-side jewel positions; dial-side keyless works; crown-wheel ring/click spring; explicit arbors; visible shock setting/regulator indication; constructive bridge holes; tapered gear teeth; and a dedicated escape-wheel primitive.

M3c — mesh-derived train geometry

M3c derives a nominal module and pitch radius for each external train mesh from the current centre coordinates and reference tooth/leaf counts using module = 2 × centre_distance / (wheel_teeth + pinion_leaves).

What this means.

The wheels no longer merely have the right counts; their nominal pitch radii now close against the current centre distances. The centre coordinates are still reference-derived reconstruction coordinates, so this improves internal consistency without claiming ETA manufacturing CAD.

Camera-axis inspection lighting

The default light is now explicitly aligned with the camera: a directional source is placed conceptually behind the camera and aimed through the orbit target. That makes front-facing inspection predictable and avoids inverse-square brightness collapse as the camera moves.

  • Camera aligned — easiest inspection mode; follows the view axis.
  • Manual key only — preserves azimuth/elevation/distance sliders for deliberate raking light.
  • Camera + manual key — combines frontal readability with surface-relief lighting.
  • Camera intensity and manual-key intensity both extend to 700.
  • Exposure extends to 3.20×.
  • Full bright / diagnostic deliberately prioritizes visibility over atmosphere.

The scene also uses a neutral reflection environment, stronger ambient/fill, rim light and a lighter inspection backboard so metallic surfaces do not disappear into black.

Simulation presentation

The train can run at 1× real time or 10×, 60× and 300× inspection scales. The balance remains at the documented 3 Hz; accelerated modes are presentational.
