DOCUMENTATION · PROVENANCE · M5D

ETA / Unitas 6497-2

M5d moves the Swiss lever escapement from beat-fraction choreography toward reconstructed contact geometry. Pallet-face travel and tooth-to-segment clearance now constrain the half-tooth escape release that drives the downstream train.

Accuracy contract.

This remains an educational reconstruction, not manufacturing CAD or a rigid-body contact simulation. M5d introduces real geometric constraints inside the model, but its face coordinates and millimetre clearances are still reconstruction targets rather than ETA production tolerances.

Foundation retained from M3–M5c

The reconstruction keeps the pitch-constrained train, separate compound-wheel planes, staffs and jewel spans, endshake diagnostics, causal winding, hand setting, stored reserve, energy-gated runtime, event-resolved Swiss-lever sequencing, explicit pallet/safety diagnostics, and the reserve-coupled normalized oscillator introduced in M5c.

The reference train remains centre 80 teeth; third 60 / 10-leaf pinion; seconds/fourth 120 / 8-leaf pinion; escape 15 / 10-leaf pinion. A 15-tooth escape wheel gives a 24° tooth pitch and a 12° half-tooth release per beat.

M5d — contact-face calibration

The solver begins from the reconstructed escape-wheel and pallet centres. It identifies the visible 15-tooth escape-wheel phase, chooses the tooth nearest the expected pallet-contact sector, and calibrates a reconstructed entry contact face against that tooth. The exit contact face is calibrated one half-tooth later.

That means entry/exit contact is tied to the actual visible wheel phase rather than a separate arbitrary phase clock.

These millimetre values are reconstruction targets. They exist so the model can reason in contact space instead of hiding the mechanism behind magic timing constants.

Geometry-derived lock, unlock and release

M5d measures how far the active pallet face has moved away from its calibrated lock point and how far the opposite face remains from its capture point. Those spatial quantities determine the current event:

  • LOCK while the starting face remains inside the unlock clearance;
  • UNLOCK once that face has physically cleared;
  • IMPULSE while the wheel traverses the allowed half-step and the opposite face approaches;
  • DROP / APPROACH near the end of the transition;
  • LOCK again when the opposite face reaches the capture region.

Tooth-to-face contact test

The solver tracks the specific tooth expected to interact on each alternating beat. It computes the distance from that tooth tip to the reconstructed pallet-face segment. If the candidate release would push the tooth through the target face, a short one-dimensional search clamps the wheel back to the last non-penetrating solution.

Causal change.

The downstream train no longer receives the old beat-window release fraction. It receives the escape angle produced by the M5d geometry solver. That angle is converted back into released train time, so the fourth/seconds, third and centre wheels and the hands now inherit geometry-constrained escapement motion.

Live contact diagnostics

The demo now exposes geometry event, active constraint, start-face travel, target-face remaining distance, tooth-to-face gap, active escape-wheel tooth, geometry-derived release percentage and solver health.

An optional M5d overlay draws the reconstructed entry and exit solver faces, the tracked tooth, the nearest contact point and the path between the calibrated lock targets.

Exploded view versus mechanical coordinates

The solver intentionally runs in assembled movement design coordinates. Exploded-view offsets are presentation transforms and do not alter the mechanical contact solution. Assemble the watch when you want the contact overlay and visible parts to coincide most clearly.

M5c oscillator dynamics retained

Reserve still affects a normalized barrel-drive proxy; discrete impulse packets replenish the balance; damping removes amplitude; and a low-amplitude oscillator can stall while residual reserve remains. M5d adds a geometry-health gate after that amplitude gate so an invalid contact reconstruction cannot silently keep consuming reserve.

What M5d still does not claim

  • measured ETA pallet-face coordinates or exact escape-tooth polygons;
  • production lock depth, draw, drop or banking-pin placement;
  • continuous polygon collision manifolds or rigid-body dynamics;
  • friction, lubrication, elastic deformation, rebound or impact physics;
  • measured pallet efficiency, balance inertia or hairspring stiffness;
  • amplitude-dependent rate error or positional timing effects.

Inspection workflow

Wind the watch, choose the Escapement view, assemble the movement for direct contact inspection, and enable Show M5d solver faces / contact point. Use 0.1× or 0.25× for slow motion, or 0× paused with event/beat stepping for static inspection.

Next work

M5e — amplitude / rate coupling

Let oscillator amplitude perturb simulated rate rather than remaining perfectly nominal at 3 Hz, while keeping nominal specification and simulated instantaneous rate clearly separate.

M5f — deeper geometric contact

Replace tooth-tip/segment distance with reconstructed tooth and pallet polygons, derive lock depth/drop from polygon intersections, and migrate reconstruction targets toward measured reference dimensions where possible.

M6 — shared system simulation

Separate barrel-arbor winding from drum release, improve force transmission through the train, and couple reserve, impulse, amplitude and rate in one shared mechanical state.
