M3f foundation — true-pitch train and stepped staffs

M3f replaced the older decorative gear-radius convention with a train-specific construction whose supplied radius has exact pitch-circle semantics. The tooth profile remains educational rather than manufacturing involute/cycloidal CAD, but the rendered wheel/pinion engagement and the solved pitch circles now belong to the same geometric model.

The large wheel bodies sit on separate bridge-side planes and each driven pinion occupies the plane of the wheel that drives it. Uniform presentation rods were replaced with stepped staffs running between the mainplate and bridge jewel regions.

M3g — bearing stack and endshake

M3g adds explicit bearing-shape cues to the centre, third, seconds/fourth and escape staffs: conical pivot noses, wheel-seat shoulders, narrowed bridge-side pivot regions, and a visible jewel-to-jewel relationship.

Reconstruction endshake targets

Centre wheel0.040 mm
Third wheel0.040 mm
Seconds/fourth0.040 mm
Escape wheel0.035 mm
These are reconstruction targets.

They are deliberately not labeled as ETA production tolerances. Their purpose is to model the concept of controlled axial play and force the current bridge/mainplate stack to leave a believable amount of room for it.

Endshake inspection

Show endshake guides marks each wheel’s tiny nominal axial travel and links that working range to the reconstructed jewel regions. The optional motion control provides 1×, 10× and 25× amplitudes; the latter two are explicitly exaggerated diagnostic views so the movement can be inspected on a screen.

M3g — wheel-body separation

The large wheels can overlap heavily in front projection because the actual train is wheel → small coaxial pinion → wheel. M3g now treats the axial separation of the large wheel bodies as a first-class diagnostic instead of relying on visual intuition.

Show wheel-body gap guides exposes the thin z-separation between adjacent wheel bodies. This is separate from the pitch/contact guide, which shows where the actual wheel and driven pinion mesh.

Train-stack inspection

The Train stack camera preset turns the movement nearly side-on and enables staff/clearance, endshake and wheel-body-gap diagnostics together. This is the most useful view for judging whether the current procedural reconstruction is physically coherent rather than merely attractive from the bridge side.

Lighting

The balanced camera-axis setup remains the default: moderate camera-aligned illumination, manual raking controls, and a deliberately excessive Full bright / diagnostic preset when geometry becomes difficult to read.
