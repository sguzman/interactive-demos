DOCUMENTATION · PROVENANCE · M6D

ETA / Unitas 6497-2

M6d closes the current normalized movement architecture. Barrel torque, dynamic train reaction load, finite escapement work, integrated balance state, release timing, reserve and explicit stall modes now participate in one causal loop rather than merely sharing animation time.

Accuracy contract.

The official reference remains 3 Hz / 21,600 A/h, 17 jewels, 44° lift angle, and 53 h minimum / 60 h typical reserve. M6 torque, load, efficiency, spring-turn, work and stall values are educational reconstruction parameters—not ETA production measurements.

Closed-loop causal chain

crown → arbor winding → spring twist → barrel torque → dynamic train load → transmission → available escapement work → finite polygon work transfer → delivered Δω → integrated balance θ/ω → pallet / escape release → train → hands

Downstream demand also feeds back upstream: rejected work, low amplitude, geometry blockage and stall state increase reaction load, reducing drive margin and transmission.

M6a — arbor / drum power separation

M6a distinguishes crown/ratchet-driven barrel-arbor winding from slow barrel-drum release. Reserve becomes normalized spring twist; that twist feeds a reconstructed torque curve; load and transmission reduce that torque before it reaches the escapement work layer.

Modeled full drum release8 turns
Low-twist knee0.12
Torque plateau0.74 / 0.22 / 0.04

The visible barrel drum now rotates from the separate release state while the arbor side remains the winding / click-held side.

M6b — dynamic reaction load

M6b replaces the old fixed train load with a smoothed reaction-load target. It includes baseline running demand plus contributions from recent delivered escapement work, rejected work/backpressure, low oscillator amplitude, unhealthy geometry and stalled state.

Conceptually:

drive margin = spring torque − dynamic train load

post-load drive = positive drive margin × load-dependent transmission

That post-load drive becomes the available budget for M5i's finite-surface work-transfer calculation.

Base reaction load≈ 0.075
Nominal transmission≈ 94%
Torque-margin hold≈ 0.018

The movement can therefore stop with reserve still present when available spring torque cannot overcome modeled reaction load.

M6c — shared work ledger

Every detailed escapement opportunity is now reconciled using one normalized bookkeeping chain:

spring-side budget − train/load loss − contact loss − balance-delivered work = ledger residual

The UI exposes the residual directly. The current target is numerical closure around zero.

This is not physical energy in joules. It is an explicit consistency contract between the modeled spring, train, contact and oscillator layers.

M6c also exposes a normalized spring-differential turn state. Full twist currently maps to 8 relative presentation turns; that is not a measured 6497-2 mainspring active-turn count.

M6d — movement-level coordinator

M6d reads barrel, load feedback, work ledger, polygon contact, oscillator and power-gate state as one movement and exposes explicit operating modes such as:

  • UNWOUND;
  • WINDING;
  • RUNNING;
  • WINDING WHILE RUNNING;
  • PAUSED;
  • TORQUE STALL;
  • GEOMETRY BLOCKED;
  • OSCILLATOR STALLED;
  • ESCAPEMENT HELD.

Torque-stall hysteresis

To avoid threshold chatter, torque stall is hysteretic. It enters when drive margin falls below approximately 0.018 normalized and remains latched until winding or load relief raises the margin above approximately 0.055.

System diagnostics

The M6d panel reports operating mode, torque-stall latch, normalized balance-energy proxy, end-to-end delivery fraction, modeled total-loss fraction, torque/load ratio, reserve and ledger closure.

M5 escapement causality retained

M6 supplies upstream mechanics; it does not replace the M5 escapement. The live movement still uses integrated θ/ω balance state, finite escape-tooth and pallet-jewel polygons, geometry-derived lock/impulse/drop/capture, polygon surface-follow distance, variable work-scaled Δω, and geometry-derived escape release as the downstream train's timing source.

What is now genuinely coupled

At the normalized educational level the simulation now connects winding input, spring state, barrel torque, arbor/drum roles, dynamic reaction load, transmission, finite escapement contact, available/delivered/lost work, oscillator angular state, torque/geometry/amplitude stall states, escape release, wheel-train progress and displayed time. Crucially, downstream mechanical demand now feeds back into upstream load.

What remains approximate

  • barrel torque is dimensionless rather than measured N·mm;
  • mainspring active-turn geometry and torque-deflection data are not measured;
  • per-mesh wheel/pinion inertia and tooth-force vectors are not solved;
  • pivot, jewel, gear and lubrication friction are not calibrated;
  • pallet contact work remains normalized rather than joules;
  • elastic impact, contact stress and compliance are not solved;
  • balance inertia and hairspring stiffness / terminal geometry are not calibrated;
  • positional, regulator and temperature effects are not yet part of the dynamic system;
  • production amplitude and timing performance are not claimed.

The important distinction is that these are now mostly calibration and fidelity problems attached to an established causal architecture rather than missing subsystem connections.

Inspection workflow

Assemble the movement and wind it in the Winding view. Watch spring twist and torque rise. Then switch to Escapement, enable the finite tooth/jewel overlay, and run at 0.1× or 0.25×. Compare M6b drive margin, M5i available/delivered/lost work, M6c ledger closure and M5f oscillator Δω / amplitude. The M6d system panel tells you whether the whole movement regards itself as running, paused, torque-stalled, geometry-blocked, oscillator-stalled or unwound.

Future deepening after M6

The highest-value future work is now calibration and physical fidelity: better barrel/mainspring geometry and torque data, per-mesh train inertia/friction, force/torque propagation, finite roller-jewel/fork-slot/horn/safety geometry, regulator/hairspring mechanics, positional effects, temperature response, and validation against measured 6497-2 amplitude/reserve/rate behavior when defensible data is available.
